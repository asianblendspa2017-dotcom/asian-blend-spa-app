Our specialty · Philippine Hilot

The healing hands of the Philippines, here in Cape Town.

Long before spas, every Filipino village had its manghihilot — a traditional healer whose knowledge was handed down from elder to apprentice, within families, for generations. Hilot comes from the Tagalog word for rubbing and pressing: an intuitive, hands-on art that blends massage, gentle joint alignment and energy work to bring the body back into balance.

As Cape Town's first Filipino massage clinic, Hilot is the heart of everything we do. Every session is personalised — the therapist reads your body first, then works with palms, thumbs and forearms where you actually need it, not to a fixed routine.

Ginhawa

The Filipino word for relief, ease and well-being — the feeling Hilot is meant to leave you with.

The Hilot ritual — four traditional steps
01

Pakiramdam

Feeling the body

Before any pressure, your therapist reads the body with open palms — finding where tension, tightness and lamig (trapped cold) have settled.

02

Dahon ng saging

Warm banana leaf

Warmed banana leaves are laid over the back. Traditionally, the leaf dries fastest where heat and tension gather — showing where the work is needed.

03

Langis ng niyog

Warm coconut oil

Warm virgin coconut oil and long, rhythmic palm strokes, with thumb and forearm pressure to release knots, ease the joints and restore energy flow.

04

Pahinga

Rest & restore

A slow, unhurried close with gentle stretching, so your nervous system settles before you step back into your day.
